1. Internal Review

BOLDiquette

Pre-submission says only “Submit a first full draft to the compulsory BOLD PI on your paper 10 days ahead of the final deadline.” No structured runway, no review, no roles, no checklist.

Implementation

The pre-submission run is a fixed timeline, and the first full draft goes through a simulated full peer review (venues are all similar).

  • 8 wk before the deadline — register the paper (abstract, outline, author list, target venue, compute estimate) on the Internal Review Board.
  • 6 wk — Pitch Day: every paper is pitched to the group.
  • 4 wk — first full draft → Internal Review, worked through by a junior and a senior reviewer from the pool.
  • 2 wk — final draft, every review comment incorporated.
  • 1 wk — the PI reads the final draft and approves it for submission. That is the PI’s only involvement.
  • The reviewer pool = every paper’s authors plus volunteers, with at least one senior.
  • 12-item checklist (6 Format, 6 Science; each item folds together several related points to keep the tick count manageable), on every board card’s detail page. A junior and a senior reviewer are assigned per submission on the board; each fills in the checklist. A paper can’t be marked Approved until both passes are complete.

Proposed BOLDiquette change

Add a new subsection between Pre-submission and Post-submission (drop-in prose in proposed-boldiquette-addition.md). Supersedes the old “first draft to the PI 10 days ahead” line.

New

2. Internal Review Board

BOLDiquette

Nothing.

Implementation

The Internal Review Board, one board per venue + year. Columns mirror the guide’s 9 phases, named past tense for the checkpoint already met to be sitting there: Registered → Pitched → Drafted → Reviewed → PI Approved → Submitted → Posted → Reviews Out → Accepted — each column is an attestation of a real-world checkpoint being met, not a work-in-progress bucket. Within Drafted a card is colour- and badge-coded In review / Changes requested / Approved (derived from the checklist, plus a reviewer-settable flag); the outcomes Accepted / Rejected / Withdrawn are a separate badge, not to be confused with the Accepted column (the final draft actually reaching the venue’s own decision). Per-card fields: compute estimate; post-submission — submission link (OpenReview or other), rebuttal deadline, rebuttal doc link, review notes. The venue carries the call for papers and submission-page links, the paper deadline, an optional explicit Pitch Day, and its own dates — reviews released and rebuttal deadline as standard fields, abstract / notification / camera-ready as optional — plus page limit and anonymity. From the paper deadline the board derives and clearly displays one chronological deadline list — register (−8 wk), Pitch Day (explicit, or −6 wk), first draft (−4 wk), final draft (−2 wk), PI approval (−1 wk), then the venue’s own dates — and exports the whole list as a downloadable .ics.

Proposed BOLDiquette change

Covered by the same draft addition plus its “Addition to Post-submission” block.

Known limitation (not a BOLDiquette question): status changes are a free-form dropdown, except the Approved gate (both checklist passes must be complete). Who may move a card / tick which pass is unenforced — see TODO.md Permissions.

4. Pre-submission timeline

Implementation is ahead of BOLDiquette.

BOLDiquette

Register 4 weeks ahead; first full draft to the PI 10 days ahead; clear your calendar for the 4 weeks before the deadline.

Implementation

The real timeline (per Mattie, who ran this before) — 8 wk abstract + outline + compute → registered · 6 wk Pitch Day · 4 wk first full draft → Internal Review · 2 wk final draft with all comments in · 1 wk PI approval · 0 submit.

Proposed BOLDiquette change

Replace the Pre-submission timing lines with this runway.

8. “On Acceptance” step dropped

BOLDiquette (On Acceptance)

“Submit the form to add the paper to our website.”

Implementation

The guide has no “On Acceptance” phase. Once BOLD OS is live the project (and its publications) already exist there, and an accepted publication is published to the website automatically — see TODO.md.

10. Pre-Conference networking

BOLDiquette (Pre-Conference)

Poster, practice talk, slides format, example posters, “posters are not papers”. No networking guidance.

Implementation (Phase 9)

Adds two items — email people you want to meet at the conference at least 3 weeks beforehand to set up a meeting; and attend as many of the conference’s social events as you can (mandatory), since they’re usually the best networking.

Proposed BOLDiquette change

Add both to Pre-Conference, including the mandatory framing on social events.

Divergence

11. What a pitch contains

BOLDiquette (Research Project Lifecycle, Collective Brainstorm)

An “XYZ format description of what we are planning to do and why”, and a “Proof-of Concept experiment that can validate the approach”, with a hypothesis for what you expect to find.

Implementation

The Pitch a project guide (Write the pitch) asks for seven sections, in order: area of research; problem and motivation (X); why should we solve it (X); why is it hard to solve (Y); intuition and hypothesis (Z); draft of the method (Z); how do we verify it, i.e. the experiment plan, starting with the proof-of-concept experiment. The Project pitch skill builds slides with the same sections.

Proposed BOLDiquette change

Replace the XYZ line with the seven sections, or with a link to the Pitch a project guide.

13. Research Project Lifecycle reorganised

BOLDiquette

A “Remember:” list first (post in #all-active-projects, present at group meeting, proj-NAME channel, add a PI, meeting notes and master slides, provenance), then Think → Collective Brainstorm → Run the experiments → (Sometimes) Reflect → Open source EVERYTHING → (optionally) Start a company.

Implementation

The Pitch a project guide has Think → Brainstorm together → Write the pitch → Start the project (the “Remember:” list) → Run the proof of concept → Reflect, sometimes. Open source EVERYTHING and Start a company are left out, as they come after the pitch. Wording is lightly edited throughout; Jakob’s first-person “I have commonly returned to projects and ideas after a long time to then see them succeed” became “Projects often succeed after a long break.”

Proposed BOLDiquette change

None needed if BOLDiquette links to the guide for these steps (see 14); otherwise reconcile the order.

14. How to ML Paper and How to ML Rebuttal copied into the site

BOLDiquette (ML Conference Cycle)

Implementation

The Write a paper and Write a rebuttal guides are copies of those docs, lightly edited, split into parts, crediting their authors (the rebuttal guide keeps its CC BY-NC licence). They are edited on the site, through proposed changes, and don’t follow later edits to the Google Docs.

Point those links (and the Research Project Lifecycle steps) to the site’s guides, so each text has one version.

Divergence

15. Who changes the BOLDiquette

BOLDiquette (Modification)

Anyone suggests edits; BOLD-exec holds edit rights.

Implementation

Nobody edits it directly. Every change, a PI’s or admin’s included, is a proposed change, and a PI or admin other than whoever proposed it accepts or declines it.

Proposed BOLDiquette change

Say that every change, BOLD-exec’s too, is accepted by a second member of BOLD-exec.
