Preamble / TLDR

TL;DR “How To Work with Us”

  • Any questions not covered here?

    • 1: Ask your peers.
    • 2: Ping @Paula Leite on slack or find her in person at BOLD HQ :)
    • 3: Last resort: Ask us.
    • Important:
      • Should the question be covered here? Add it here with the answer you found!

  • Contact with us:

    • Short sentences on slack for quick check-ins / sanity checks or quick feedback
    • Be in the office (BOLD HQ) for informal check-ins on a regular basis.
    • Use free slots on our calendar for anything substantial

  • Key points of input from us:

    • Early project scoping.
    • Project supervision once things are up and running (ideally part of a bigger “pan” meeting – Make most of every chance to get collective feedback).
    • Paper planning / feedback (best via in-person writing session).

  • Come to us with results:

    • Try to get some result every week.

    • Bring slides and graphs that you prepare in advance

      • Plot as many relevant statistics as appropriate (graphs are invaluable for diagnosing what went wrong and understanding what went right. One graph is worth many words)
      • This doesn’t replace having your own interpretation but allows us to sanity check.

    • Take notes of todos and recap 30s at the beginning of the meeting how the results relate to what we discussed

    • Negative results are super useful. If we make a suggestion and it doesn’t work, let us know. Figure out why things don’t work.

  • Note:

    • If you are making progress, we will have your project running in our mental background process, and will result in better supervision
    • If you say you’re going to xyz, do xyz. Be dependable.
    • Not doing all of the above wastes time, and risks us mentally disengaging.

  • Now you read the TL;DR (👏 👏👏), please find time to read the rest - it’s a bad look to ask us questions that are addressed here. It’s a much worse look to violate expectations and rules set out here.

The BOLDiquette: What / Why etc (i.e. provenance and self-improvement)

  • Vision - the BOLDiquette is our first big step towards the BOLD Operating System. A self-referential, self-improving set of norms, processes, and policies that both allow BOLDies to be productive / happy but also ultimately provide provenance to the research work we do, i.e. how we do it, and why we do it like this.

  • Style - The tone is rather abrupt since (as you know) we like clarity. Think of this as our collective constitution and (verbal) operating system.

  • Modification:

    • Everything here can be edited (suggested change) by anyone.

      • If anything is unclear, let us know via comment.
      • If there are parts you disagree with let us know via comments.
      • This is BOLD internal guidance. Wherever it conflicts with the Universities’ guidance, the latter takes precedent (but please highlight the conflict in this doc via comment).
      • Please sign into your google account to make suggested edits.
      • If your suggested edit is controversial, a maintainer of the doc will start a new tab in this document (“request for comment”) to discuss the change.

    • Currently BOLD-exec holds edit rights. In the long term we plan to have a wikipedia-like structure for edits, approvals, and governance.

  • If you don’t flag disagreement, we’ll expect you to follow the BOLDiquette.

  • Note: BOLDiquette is ground truth. A slack message, spoken conversation or other does not trump it.

  • Enforcement - much of the BOLDiquette is about norms and behaviours which are socially enforceable by all BOLDies on a daily basis. Other rules (e.g. publication, CoI, Internship policy) are self-revealing if violated.

  • Implementation - the BOLDiquette is also the design-doc for the BOLD OS. Whenever the document is used to produce downstream code / artifacts it’s crucial to keep the provenance in place. Specifically, make sure that the code / artifact transparently refers back to the BOLDiquette as the ground truth and as the place to make any changes to the functionality or process.

  • Provenance – the BOLDiquette is an evolution of the FLAIRiquette (the internal guidance for FLAIR) which itself has been adopted / expanded upon as a blueprint by a number of ML labs (Adam Mahdi, Christian Schroeder De Witt, Paul Rottger, Fazl Barez..). Google docs has a complete edit history for both the FLAIRiquette and the changes since - we will make these publicly available.

  • Hello there :) – if you use the BOLDiquette as a starting point for your own lab or otherwise, please add a pointer back here.

BOLD NORMS, VALUES, AND GOALS

Our goals for BOLD:

  • At the core BOLD is about two bets:

    • Paradigm changing breakthroughs in AI that differentially improve the chances of a pluralistic, distributed, open-source AI stack which serves and empowers humanity.
    • New modes of operation for AI research in academia that are more focussed, better resourced, collaborative, agile, and transparent than existing models. These will both allow us to make these paradigm changing breakthroughs but also act as blueprints for others to follow.

  • We summarise our outputs as 4 Ts (the 40s ;):

    • Technology:

      • We ship our methods and algorithms as useful technology for the world.

    • Tools:

      • We develop tooling for empowering the open-source AI research stack.

    • Teams:

      • Each paper / research project is a chance for a world-class team of people to find each other and work together. This should ideally result in company creation where appropriate.

    • Templates:

      • BOLD is a blueprint for others to follow to re-boot and re-focus academic research.
      • We will publish and open-source all documentation to facilitate this (including this document)

  • Specifically “success” means:

    • # of ‘test-of-time’ awards (i.e.10 years in hindsight) at major ML conferences
    • # of unicorns coming out of the BOLD ecosystem in 10 years time.
    • # of research organisations adopting the BOLD blueprint and other templates.
    • # of users of our tools.

  • We also:

  1. Train the next generation of AI scientists

  2. Make progress on the hardest problems, e.g.:

    1. What is consciousness and intelligence?
    2. How do we create self-improving systems?
    3. What does learning in a world full of learning agents look like?

  3. Do all of this while limiting risks:

    1. Students need to graduate, so we need to balance ambitious projects with incremental, lower risk progress
    2. Lights need to (ideally) stay on and it’s hard to get funding for the most speculative research [update: We managed to do this, now let’s deliver].

BOLD Principles

  • TransparencyMAXXING:

    • IP does not exist. Teams and technology do. Research best practice does exist. Norms and values exist.
    • All of BOLD runs on open-source and open-science. Therefore, no IP can be generated through BOLD.
    • If you would like to start a company you first open-source everything you have done under the BOLD hat with a permissive license.

  • “Membrane free organisation” (credit to Irina Haivas for the term):

    • anyone can start working with us where interests and values align
    • If you want to work with us just show up and do the work.
    • Gain trust by shipping, shaping, and contributing.

  • One consistent collaboration layer across all different universities – everything outlined here works consistently and seamlessly across all the partnering / executing Universities (currently Oxford / UCL / Imperial).

  • Self-referential / self-improvement based processes [i.e. this document]

General BOLDiquette:

  • Be benevolent and supportive towards others.
  • Play an active part in shaping BOLD and helping it succeed – be the change you’d like to see in the world.
  • Assume that others are benevolent and supportive. If you observe behaviour that is inconsistent with these guidelines, address it ASAP. Nobody is perfect and feedback is gold. Try to keep this factual: “I observed X Y Z and am struggling to make sense of it.. Please help me.”
  • Be inclusive in your actions and words. Please make sure that people around you feel comfortable and heard. Champion the quieter voices in the room. For example, if you are in a meeting and you notice that someone was trying to make a point but it got lost in a larger discussion, bring it back up: “I liked what X said and I agree with it…”, etc.
  • It’s per-se ok to ask someone out if you are confident there is mutual chemistry. However, anything but a “yes!” means “no!”, so please don’t ask twice or do anything else that might cause discomfort.
  • Praise and complement others! Academia can be a very, very critical environment and being critical is useful in many situations but please remember to help others build confidence by being positive and calling out the good, whether in public or private.
  • Remember that everyone is better-off if we collectively grow the pie than if we spend time and head-space dividing it :) Attribution is linear (and zero-sum) but the size of the pie is exponential (and open-ended!).
  • We are not perfect, but we take feedback to heart and have learned a lot in the process. As a rule, we say “thank you” for feedback and see what we can learn from it, even if we disagree. Remember, we are always open to and grateful for in-person or anonymous feedback.
  • Learn to give feedback, even if the message is not easy. Make sure you frame feedback appropriately (e.g. “I’d like to help you succeed.. “ or “.. to help BOLD be awesome.. “)

Labiquette:

  • Be mindful of others around the lab when discussing. Close the door gently and remove the magnet as the last person out.
  • If you are having a chat that goes beyond a few minutes and / or doesn’t require you sitting in front of your screen, please move to the social area / meeting rooms to avoid distracting people around you. Some people’s voices ‘carry’ – with great power comes great responsibility. Use it rarely and responsibly.
  • Don’t disrupt people when they are “in the zone” to ask for advice.
  • English is the lingua-franca, please stick to it unless everyone around is fluent in the alternative language.
  • Avoid using your phone or computer in group meetings unless you are taking notes.
  • Keep the common areas tidy.
  • Please don’t come to the office if you are feeling sick / contagious. Let others know you are taking the day off or working from home.

Collaboration Etiquette:

  • TL;DR: Be accountable and don’t waste people’s time.

  • Calendar invites are ground-truth: If you accept an invite, we expect you to be there. Be on (Oxford) time - by default meetings start 5min past the hour to give time to arrive.

  • Take notes. If a meeting is not in our Google calendar, it won’t happen. If there are no notes, it didn’t happen and we will think twice before having another meeting with you. Maximise common knowledge (see SLACKiquette above)

  • If you need time with us, here are our booking links:

    • Jakob:

      • 15 min – quick check-ins only need agenda
      • 30 min – long meetings need an agenda and a slide deck. Please book two 15 min slots.

    • bold-exec@googlegroups.com

  • If you meet with someone, be prepared for the meeting:

    • Come with an agenda, ideally slides:

      • Reminder Next steps as set out at the last meeting
      • Progress since last meeting and new results
      • Open Issues
      • Plans for next week

    • If you agree to next steps, report back at the next meeting on the progress towards these next steps. If you haven’t made progress, explain why.

    • This also holds for supervision meetings and internal meetings with other BOLDies. E.g. If you don’t take at least 20 minutes to prepare for our 1:1s you are likely not making most of them.

  • If you ask for a meeting with anyone for anything and they make a suggestion for your project and you tell them “this is good / interesting / relevant”, report back. Let them know if you did it and what happened.

  • If anyone helps you (e.g. by introducing you to someone), let them know the outcome and thank them. Always close the loop.

  • Include anyone who made relevant contributions to your project on the paper. Always. Even if it’s someone whom you just talk to for an hour – if it substantially changed or improved the paper, they are an author (or at least should be offered to be one).

  • If you have repeated feedback from someone, or meetings with them, on your paper, offer them to be an author. This is particularly relevant for working with postdocs.

  • If ideas originate from a joint discussion, offer everyone involved to join the project.

  • Follow the Project-lifecycle points above

  • PS: There is little point in trying to get onto as many (mediocre) papers as possible. Find good close collaborators and do great work – that’s what’ll make the difference. Cherish your close collaborations. Good collaborators are hard to find. See the “Collaboration” section above. Use each paper as a chance to find the dream team.

(Co)-Supervision:

  • We are strong believers in tight collaboration across all of BOLD and having co-supervision is a great way to foster this.
  • To be clear, co-supervision here means being supervised by two supervisors rather than alternatingly being supervised by one and then the other on different projects.
  • What that means practically, is that we expect you to involve both of your supervisors in all of your projects.
  • There might be occasions where it makes sense for one supervisor to decide to take a step back from a specific project, but that is on them / us, rather than you.

On Collaborations:

  • Are they encouraged? Absolutely!! A PhD can be very challenging and having close collaborators is one of the best ways of making sure that you make it through happily, fulfilled and successfully while also making friendships that might last a lifetime.

  • Collaboration is also a great way of de-risking projects: You can parallelise exploration along a few axes and then jointly focus on making the most promising direction work as a team. You can EXPLORE AND EXPLOIT.

  • There are very different types of collaborations. At a very high level, we recommend to have three types on each project:

    • Partner-in-crime: This is what it says – a second person who is hands-on with ideation, code, and experiments. You jointly own the project and both focus this specific project ahead of the conference deadline. If you look at our Google Scholar, DiCE is the only single author paper from Jakob’s Phd. And even this was a close call :)
    • Advisory: this person is ideally someone who has direct relevant experience in the specific area of research and is available for at least 30 minutes a week to provide guidance on the overall project and progress etc. For junior Phd students this is particularly relevant and often should not be their supervisors (i.e. us :) )
    • Supervisor: The senior academic whose reputation is ultimately on the line :) Each BOLD project (i.e. where one of the first authors is a member of BOLD) needs to have at least one BOLD PI in this position.

  • A few things:

    • “BOLD First” – before reaching out to external collaborators, have a think if there’s someone internal who is well suited. E.g. present your idea at the group meeting asking for collaborators. (if you are co-supervised and have a second home then this rule includes your second home)
    • “BOLD-Friends Second” – our Slack has a great pool of “Friends-of-BOLD” who have established collaborations with the group. If you struggle to find someone, let me know.
    • External – last but always an option: If there are good reasons to collaborate externally do it. It’s very unlikely that you’ll be able to establish the kind of in depth 24/7 “partner in crime collaboration” with someone external.

  • Visitors:

    • 1st step: Match potential candidate with a Phd student, Fellow, or Postdoc at BOLD. Every visitor should be working closely with someone at BOLD who is not us.

    • 2nd step: Approach us :) we can usually do this.

    • Restrictions:

      • Current Undergraduates need to have work permit in the UK or get their own visa
      • For postgraduates (Phd student and up) this is not a requirement.

    • It takes about 3-4 weeks to set up.

    • Usually capped at six months.

  • Caution:

    • It’s easy to think that with collaboration more is better. I.e. you might be tempted to seek advice from a lot of external sources. Don’t fall for it – nothing replaces in-depth thought and continuous discussion with a small group of close collaborators. In other words, make sure you know what you think (and why) and worry less about the superficial opinion you get from someone based on a one-off discussion, even if that person seems impressive.
    • Industry has become a sinkhole for ideas, so please exercise caution when discussing your ideas with anyone not at BOLD.
    • Specifically, if you exchange ideas with people in academia there is a tacit understanding: should there be a fruitful discussion, anyone who is involved in the ideation is invited to contribute to the project. Industry labs often don’t allow external collaborators, so what happens is that ideas are taken and implemented behind closed doors. A one way street.
    • Industry labs often also contractually bind employees to secrecy, so they often won’t share good ideas with you. Another one way street.
    • Treat all ideas and suggestions from fellow BOLDies as strictly BOLD internal unless specifically agreed otherwise.

Our commitments to BOLDies:

  1. Transparency - e.g by publishing our mode of operation via this document.
  2. Support via regular supervision. Usually this will be either 30 min or 1h per week in the first year and then based on needs / individual situation. Note: You need to be proactive in scheduling this.
  3. Career advice / mentorship / making introductions.
  4. In-depth feedback on papers (when submitted inline with internal deadline) and support during the conference process.
  5. Maintaining a supportive and optimistic outlook.

Useful Information and Advice

General advice:

  • Disconnect. We encourage you to spend a few hours per week without any external inputs. Go for a walk, run, evensong or similar and (crucially) leave your devices at home. Think and reflect. Most of our “good” ideas originate from periods of disconnection.

  • Disconnect. We recommend not to use Twitter et al outside of promoting your own work (and even then it might be better not to).

    • Exposing ourselves to reward shaping in social media (e.g. likes / retweets) can have a detrimental effect both on mental health and long term quality of the work.
    • Carefully using Twitter as a tool is probably the best way to go, but requires a lot of discipline since it’s also a highly addictive product.
    • A good safety guard is to keep your feed as “following” rather than “for you”. That disables the algorithm and gives you full control.

  • Disconnect. Take time away from the agents. Practice thinking like you practice running.

  • Reflect. Why do you want to do a Phd? If we manage to remember what we want it’s a lot easier to say no to things we don’t want.

  • Enjoy. Being allowed to pursue open-ended science for curiosity sake is a huge privilege and often a wonderful journey. Make most of it.

PhD/DPhil/MPhil done, what now?

  • At a very high level there are usually three paths for BOLDies to consider once they are ready to leave the nest:

    • Academia
    • Fundamental Research in a tech lab
    • Applied research in “industry”
    • Startup - do this!

  • It’s usually a good idea to start thinking about which path you might want to take somewhat early in your DPhil/MPhil, e.g. 1.5 years into the process.

  • However, there is a huge caveat: All of these routes are much easier to pursue if you do innovative, high impact work. So focus on that, all else will follow.

BOLD RESEARCH PROCESSES

Research Project Lifecycle:

  • Remember:

    • Post in #all-active-projects ASAP

    • Present in BOLD Group Meeting ASAP

    • Create a private project slack channel (“proj-NAME”)

      • Add at least one BOLD PI to the channel as the BOLD supervisor.

    • Keep all eyes on target:

      • BOLD IS LOOKING FOR PARADIGM BREAKING INNOVATION

    • A recurring meeting should have a living notes document.

      • Here’s Jakob’s personal guide that served him well.
      • Remember to link out the meeting-notes document from the calendar invite and the projects slack channel.

    • A recurring project meeting needs to have a “master slides” presentation in Google Slides. This keeps getting updated every week with the most recent slides at the beginning.

      • We start every recurring 1:1 by looking at your notes from our last meeting. If they are repeatedly absent, we might suggest we stop having 1:1s for the time being.

    • Treat all project documentation (brainstorming doc, github history, experimental logs, slack channel) as provenance of your artefact from the onset and open-source it when ready.

    • Make all documents commentable by anyone with the link.

  • Think:

    • what problems / failure modes of ML have you come across that seem intriguing? What kind of mathematical connection / model do you find exciting? What papers did you recently like? Is there anyone you’d like to work with or whose work you’d like to follow-up on? … write down some of these answers. You might also want to take inspiration from the BOLDmini-projects or the BOLD Open Actionable Problems Collections
    • You might want to guide your explorations by considering your risk appetite and time horizon. If you are early in the Phd it might make sense to find something that lends itself to a relatively “safe” conference paper for transfer, while someone with their Phd’s work finished but extra time on the clock may want to take on Moonshot projects.
    • CRUCIAL: EVALUATIONS!! Really early in your project, think about (A) how will you measure / show that you have succeeded? (B) What will be cheap-ish ways to measure progress along the way?
    • SANITY CHECK: If we succeed, will it be big news?

  • Collective Brainstorm:

    • run some of these ideas past people that might be interested. You can always do this and you can also do this with anyone. Bare in mind who is available to collaborate and in what capacity.. Ideally at this point repeated conversations and good note keeping help crystalise the project along a few axes
    • Feel free to make your estimates for risk and time horizon explicit in the discussion.
    • XYZ format description of what we are planning to do and why
    • Proof-of Concept experiment that can validate the approach. This should be something that can be done quickly. It’s good to agree on how long this should roughly take.
    • One example is distilling a complicated phenomena into a minimal example where current methods break. This is commonly a good approach for algorithmic innovations, as opposed to more Deep Learning like methods.
    • Make sure to formulate a hypothesis for what you expect to find when running the experiments.

  • Run the experiments and evaluate the results:

    • First of all – does it make sense? Do the results match what you expected to see? If not, why not? Can you distinguish between a bug or other issue vs your intuitions being off?
    • Remember to visualise more than the final rewards / loss to gain insight into the workings of the system.
    • When you meet collaborators present results clearly so that everyone can follow along.
    • If results look encouraging, start planning for the ML Conference Cycle part of the project lifecycle.

  • (Sometimes) Reflect:

    • If you have been going on a project for “a while” compared to what you had agreed the project “should” take before, it is good to take a step back and reflect whether you still believe that is a productive way to spend your time and energy.
    • This should happen as a discussion amongst the core collaborators.
    • It is entirely ok to hit Pause on a project and go back to Think or Collective Brainstorm.
    • Ideally, when pressing Pause you make it easier for a future version of yourself or others to pick up the project again or learn from the work done this far. I have commonly returned to projects and ideas after a long time to then see them succeed.
    • Pull advice from PIs and others as needed.
    • Remember that your wellbeing is the top priority and that there are many wonderful things in life and in this world that are not this one project.

  • Open source EVERYTHING

  • (optionally) Start a company to turn your project into real-life value

ML Conference Cycle:

  • Pre-submission

  • By default, we are a core-ML lab.

    • Bulk of submissions will go to ICML, NeurIPS, and ICLR. These deadlines are nicely spaced out (Feb, May, September), so you should never be far away from a deadline.
    • Specialised conferences (e.g. AAMAS) are fine if justified. AAAI is usually a last resort.

  • If you target a conference, add your project 4 weeks ahead of the deadline to the project tracker w/ appropriate links.

  • If you target a conference as a main author, we recommend you clear your schedule from anything else the four weeks before the deadline.

  • Submit a first full draft to the compulsory BOLD PI on your paper 10 days ahead of the final deadline.

  • Read and follow the “How to ML Paper” guide closely. We tend to get mildly irritated when we fix things that are pointed out in the guide ;)

  • Follow the “How to ML Rebuttal” guide for rebuttals.

  • Look after your physical and mental-well being during the process. Exercise, good sleep, good food. A Phd is a marathon, not a sprint.

  • Post-submission (Credit to Rober Grosse)

  • “Now that the deadline is past, there’s one more (hopefully easy) task for all of you. After a deadline push, I find it very useful to make a list of all the loose ends in the project. It’s important to do it very soon after the deadline while everything is still fresh in your mind. This will save a lot of time and stress later on.

  • So, sometime in the next few days, please start a Google Doc to keep track of loose ends. Try to spend about an hour on it. (Or, if you’re doing lots of extra work for the Supplemental Material deadline, build this list as you work on the Supplemental Material.)

  • Some ideas for what to include:

    • experiments you still want to run
    • surprising findings that need to be explained
    • aspects of the experimental protocol that you’re not comfortable with
    • references to prior work which you need to look at more carefully
    • things that need to be explained better
    • mathematical arguments that need to be checked or made more precise

    BOLD POLICY

    Conflict of Interest

    • All of BOLD is open-source and open-science so in the medium term everything we do is self-revealing, i.e. there are no secrets.

    • This substantially makes CoI easier since IP doesn’t exist.

    • Within BOLD project “ownership / provenance” is easy:

      • Participation in slack discussions creates a track record
      • Posts in #all-active-projects establish precedence.
      • We all collaborate and focus on the biggest pie.

    • If you wear a second hat (i.e. not BOLD):

      • It is your responsibility to maintain confidentiality as required by your second hat. Assume any information shared within BOLD is going to be published (providing appropriate credit and following the Collaboration section).

      • Since BOLD is self-revealing in the medium term, the major challenge is confidentiality and scooping in the short term

        • Don’t share BOLD projects and information outside the current scope established under WHO IS BOLD without explicit consent from the authors.

        • If you come across a BOLD project that overlaps with something you are doing with your second hat you have two options:

            1. Merge efforts - provide provenance of prior work and ask the BOLD authors to join forces. Such a merge request should generally be accepted under the principle of “growing the largest pie”.

          • (b1) Recuse yourself from the BOLD project if merger is impossible for organisational reasons or other. Please stop attending project specific meetings and don’t join the project specific channel.

          • (b2) If this conflicting work with your “non-BOLD-Hat” will lead to a publication, do the following immediately:

            • You need to register your CoI with provenance for the prior art, you only need to reveal the CoI once you publish the conflicting artifact.
            • Write down the title of the project you are recusing yourself from including your name and current time, include links to provenance of prior art.
            • Get a MD5 hash of this text and post the hash in the #Conflict-of-interest channel. This won’t reveal the information but it will create a timestamp of you registering the conflict.
            • Reply to your original CoI message whenever you publish the conflicting artifact and reveal your CoI document matching the hash.
            • Note: Do not edit the original post of the CoI, ever.

      • You cannot use BOLD resources to carry out work for your second hat.

      • You need to be transparent with others about which hat you are wearing in a given interaction.

    What is the internship policy?

    • Internships are great and explicitly encouraged within the bounds below. They are a great source for finding collaborators and extending your horizon.

    • Having said this, internships can also be highly disruptive to your collaborations at BOLD and can throw students off track. As you start thinking about internships we encourage you to be clear about your motivations and also consider the opportunity cost of potentially losing momentum on your own research agenda.

    • Please keep us in the loop/informed as you start investigating internship options.

    • A few restrictions: Maximum of 2 internships per Dphil, the default is that you “pause the clock”, i.e. extend the duration of your Dphil by the time spent on the internship unless you keep working on your DPhil.

    • Caution: Pausing the clock can make it difficult to include results in your thesis. It might also cause issues with your Visa status, so check this ahead of time.

    • If you decide to not pause the clock, then the work needs to be includable in your thesis. Therefore, the internship needs to align closely with the thesis and the supervision arrangement and your participation at BOLD needs to keep going.

    • Specifically, in this case the publication coming out of the internship needs to follow the BOLDiquette. It not only looks odd to have parts of your thesis without our involvement, but also makes it difficult for us to ensure the thesis meets our (high) scientific standards.

    • The rationale for the two “internships maximum” policy is two-fold:

      • As supervisors, it is hard for us to execute our duty of (scientific) care if a student spends a large part (or even majority) of their time away on internships.
      • The best work comes from groups working together closely over extended periods. Internships risk severely disrupting this process.

    Can I work from home/the library or do I have to be in the office every day?

    • Yes, work from wherever you want. We do believe in the value of a strong in-person culture for fostering collaborations, so you will see us in the office essentially every day. We usually come into the office around 9:30am and leave around 4:30pm
    • All members of BOLD are expected to spend 2 days a week at BOLD HQ in Oxford.

    What is the vacation policy?

    • You’ll see that we are rarely out of town during Oxford term time. It happens, but we try to minimise it.

      • Take vacations whenever you want and make sure to get “proper breaks” once in a while – they are good.
      • Try to coordinate with your collaborators and other members of the lab as much as possible
      • Let us (and collaborators) know ahead of time if meetings are skipped because you are out-of-office. Set the slack status to “vacation”.

    Can I attend a conference if I am one of the joint first authors on an accepted BOLD paper?

    • Yes, you can. Please exhaust all sources of funding (college, department, conference travel grants) before relying on BOLD funding.
    • Use other (limited) funding you have access to wisely / frugally
    • Whatever the source of funding, be sensible and frugal. E.g. as Dphil students we never had our own room during conferences but always shared it with fellow students or friends. Restrict conference travel to (joint) first authorship of full conference papers.
    • Oxford department now offers funding, UCL as well provides travel grant to PhD students without CDT scholarship (please email Laura).
    • Book flights early (within constraints of acceptance notification) to minimise costs. Email paula.leite@tss.ox.ac.uk/ eee-research-finance@ucl.ac.uk to help with logistics.
    • Workshops do not count as conference papers. They are a good way to get feedback on early work and to spread the word, but do not (by themselves) justify conference travel.
    • For papers based on internships we expect the company/host organisation to pay conference attendance. Please arrange this internally.
    • Fine-print based on WhiRL’s policy:
    • “It is your responsibility to make every effort to defray your travel costs. This includes applying on time for every form of travel support (department, college, conference, industrial, etc.) for which you are eligible. BOLD funding should be considered a backstop, used only as a last resort in the event that expenses cannot be covered from other sources. BOLD will not cover travel expenses that could reasonably have been defrayed from other sources.
    • It is your responsibility to keep travel expenses reasonable by making economical choices. You do not need to suffer undue inconvenience or discomfort in order to save a few pennies, but you should not indulge in extravagances either. In other words, you should act as if you were spending your own money. This includes but is not limited do 1) booking well in advance and researching the best price, 2) travelling economy class, 3) sharing a room at an inexpensive hotel (typically not the conference hotel), 4) using public transportation, and 5) keeping food costs reasonable.”
    • To be clear – this guideline applies independently of the source funding. Ultimately conference travel is backstopped by discretionary funding from BOLD, so if you are not planning to share a room please reconsider, no matter what the source of funding is. Obviously personal circumstances differ but sharing rooms is the default expectation.
