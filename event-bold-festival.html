BOLD Festival

3–5 August 2026 · Oxford

The inaugural BOLD Festival marked the official launch of the British Open-ended Learning and Discovery Lab — merging leading fundamental-AI labs from Oxford, UCL, and Imperial College into one focused, critical-mass research lab. Three days in Oxford: talks from world-class scientists, entrepreneurs, policymakers, and industry; workshops across BOLD's research pillars; and plenty of time to just meet people.

Provenance: BOLD Festival invites & agenda · BOLD Workshops

Day 1 — Launch Day

The Examination Schools, University of Oxford · evening moved to the University Club

  • 10:00–10:50Welcome & Announcements — introducing BOLD & partners, with opening remarks from Oxford, UCL, and Google DeepMind
  • 10:50–12:30Session 1 — Fundamental AI & Entrepreneurship. Keynote: Nathan Benaich (Air Street Capital). Panel: Mark Burnett (G-Research), Raia Hadsell (Google DeepMind), Yarin Gal (Oxford), and others
  • 13:30–15:00Session 2 — Compute & Sovereignty. Keynote: Michiel Bakker (MIT), “Europe 2031: What getting AI wrong means for us”. Panel with AMD, NVIDIA, Google Cloud, and Fractile
  • 15:45–17:30Session 3 — Opportunities in Fundamental AI. Keynotes from BOLD's own Bidipta (Oxford) and Antoine Cully (Imperial), plus Katja Hofmann (Microsoft) and Elahe Arani (Wayve)
  • 17:30–22:30Evening reception, launch party & food at the University Club

Day 2 — BOLD Workshops (BOLD and Friends)

Rhodes House, Oxford · plenary, six workshops across two tracks, BBQ & social

BenchMAXXing and Breakthroughs

Dylan Cope

What benchmarks reveal and what they obscure — and whether open-endedness fundamentally resists being benchmarked at all.

Efficient Adaptation and Embodied Learning

Theo Wolf & Jarek Liesen

How to deploy autonomous systems that adapt to the noisy, chaotic real world — sim vs. real, robotics vs. autonomous driving.

From IP to Equity, Innovation and Sovereignty

Jakob Foerster

Accelerating the UK's path to technological sovereignty in fundamental AI, without losing open science along the way.

Prediction Markets, Collective Intelligence, and the Optimal Co-Scientist

Yuhe Gao & George Nigmatulin

How dispersed private information becomes reliable collective knowledge — and what the best human–AI research collaboration looks like.

Quant 2030 — Foundation Models and Open Source in Finance

Alexandre Bismuth

Closing the gap between generative order-flow models and trading-grade tools — and what a pre-competitive, shared layer for the field could look like.

Engaging with BOLD

Tim Rocktäschel

An audience-driven Ask-Us-Anything on BOLD's collaboration principles, plus pitches for new models of collaboration and specific projects.
